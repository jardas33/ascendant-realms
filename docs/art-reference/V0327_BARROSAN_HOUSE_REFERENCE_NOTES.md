# v0.327 Barrosan House Reference Notes

## Scope

This is an architectural observation sheet for one original, non-replica
Barrosan rural house. It is reference research, not a texture or photograph
archive. No reference photographs are stored in the repository.

## References

### Ecomuseu de Barroso — Pitões das Júnias

- Organisation: Ecomuseu de Barroso
- Title: Pitões das Júnias
- URL: https://www.ecomuseu.org/index/pt-pt/visite/freguesias/pitoes-das-junias
- Observed: a high, exposed mountain settlement; communal rural life; direct
  relationship between village, mountain water, stone and working land.
- Used: compact massing, weather-exposed siting, restrained rural silhouette,
  and a building that belongs to a terrain rather than sitting on a blank pad.
- Not copied literally: no named building, photograph, façade, or decorative
  detail is reproduced.

### Ecomuseu de Barroso — Corte do Boi

- Organisation: Ecomuseu de Barroso
- Title: Ecomuseu de Barroso — Corte do Boi
- URL: https://www.ecomuseu.org/index/pt-pt/visite/patrimonio
- Observed: the rural museum occupies a former cattle court; the lower level
  carries working/agricultural meaning while the upper level carries domestic
  and communal meaning.
- Used: a legible agricultural ground floor with a domestic upper level and
  modest storage storytelling.
- Not copied literally: no museum layout or historic structure is recreated.

### Município de Montalegre — Património Construído / A Casa Barrosã

- Organisation: Município de Montalegre
- Title: Montalegre, Património Construído — A Casa Barrosã
- URL: https://www.cm-montalegre.pt/cmmontalegre/uploads/document/file/44/LivroMontalegre.pdf
- Observed: local granite is a defining construction material; the rural
  farmer's house commonly combines ground and upper floors, with animal or
  storage functions below and domestic rooms above, often with a street-facing
  balcony and a large door toward an inner yard.
- Used: dominant irregular granite masonry, two-level compact massing, an
  agricultural door, a recessed upper entry, and a restrained landing.
- Not copied literally: the house is a new composition with different
  proportions, openings, stairs, and storytelling objects.

### Património Cultural, I.P. — vernacular mixed masonry reference

- Organisation: Património Cultural, I.P.
- Title: Conjunto dos Armazéns do Monte Travesso
- URL: https://imovel.patrimoniocultural.gov.pt/detalhes.php?code=7849205
- Observed: vernacular northern construction can combine granite at corners
  and openings with darker infill masonry; agricultural storage floors may be
  simple, robust and materially distinct from living floors.
- Used: heavier corner quoins, deep lintels/sills, darker foundation stone,
  and a visibly useful lower floor.
- Not copied literally: the gold asset is not a reconstruction of this site.

## Architectural lock for the asset

- Granite is the dominant structural identity, with subdued grey-brown value
  and darker wet/foundation stone near the ground.
- The footprint is compact and slightly irregular, with a heavy base and a
  clear agricultural lower floor.
- Openings are few, small and deeply recessed; the doors are timber rather
  than decorative plaster shapes.
- The roof is dark slate, pitched and visibly built from overlapping courses,
  with a restrained eave and a grounded stone chimney.
- A granite stair and small landing provide useful vertical circulation without
  turning into a copied veranda or ornamental balcony.
- Repair patches, firewood, a trough and a worn doorstep provide quiet human
  use without fantasy decoration.

## Explicit exclusions

The design excludes Japanese or Chinese roof silhouettes, pagoda eaves,
decorative exposed timber grids over white plaster, copied v0.238 veranda
railings, ornamental façade beams, unrelated tile roofs, and symmetrical
fantasy-village cottage language.
