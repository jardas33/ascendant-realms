# ASCENDANT REALMS: The Saga of the Seventy-Seventh Ascension

*World and story bible. The in-game campaign, faction, hero and unit text are built from this file (see `scripts/game/campaign_defs.gd`, `game_data.gd`, `unit_defs.gd`).*

---

## 0. The one-paragraph pitch

High in the Terras Frias, the cold granite highlands of Barroso, the land remembers. Every hearth fire and funeral, every bull-fight between rival villages, every oath shouted across a valley sinks into the ground and becomes **Lume**: living fire made of memory. Once every seventy-seven years the Lume **ascends**. It rises through springs, mines and standing stones and chooses one mortal to carry it, a **Jardas**. Whichever realm holds the Jardas holds the power to remake the world, and every Ascension has ended in war. The realms that won those wars called themselves the *Ascendant Realms*. This is the seventy-seventh Ascension. The Lume has chosen a farmhand from the village of Salto. There is one problem nobody tells them for a very long time: **the night the Lume chose them was the night they died.**

---

## 1. Why the game is called *Ascendant Realms*

The name finally means something.

- **The Ascension** is the Lume rising, every 77 years, to choose a Jardas.
- **The Ascendant Realms** are the kingdoms that won past Ascensions: the Aurean Dominion in the south, the old Castro kings, the Mouras beneath the springs. Each ruled for a generation, and each was undone by the next Ascension.
- **The Ascendant** is also the player's hero, who rose from death. The title is the twist hiding in plain sight on the box.

The wordmark stays. If a subtitle is ever wanted, the strongest options are **Ascendant Realms: The Drowned Oath** or **Ascendant Realms: Wine of the Dead**.

## 2. The world: Terras Frias

The saga lives in a fictional highland shaped by Barroso: granite villages, communal ovens, smokehouses, stone granaries on stilts, wolf-traps in the hills, wild garrano ponies, long-horned Barrosã cattle, witch-nights, and the dead walking in procession with candles. It is not Portugal. It is Portugal's cold north remembered by a fire.

| Place | Inspired by | What it is in the saga |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Salto** | Salto, Montalegre | The player's village. Granite houses, the communal oven, the Spring of the Seven Mouths where the Lume first touched the player. |
| **Montalto** | Montalegre castle | The old keep of the Barrosan Clans. Its tower is older than the kingdom. Once a year, on a Friday the 13th, the village holds the Night of the Witches there. |
| **Pitões of the Junias** | Pitões das Júnias | A monastery ruin at the edge of the world, where the Granitborn keep their records. |
| **Tourém** | Tourém | A border village that trades with everyone and trusts no one. Smugglers' paths run under it. |
| **The Carvalhelhos Castro** | Castro de Carvalhelhos | The hillfort of the Granitborn, older than any road. |
| **The Larouco** | Serra do Larouco | The winter mountain. The Careto Host dances on its snow. |
| **The Drowned Valley of Furna** | Vilarinho da Furna | A village drowned under a dam. Its roofs still show when the water is low. It is the birthplace of the Vorthak. |
| **The Rabagão Wall** | Alto Rabagão dam | The Aurean Dominion's greatest dam, and the final siege of Act IV. |
| **The Geira** | The Roman Via Nova | An ancient road of milestones. Every milestone is a sleeping Lume-stone. |
| **Boticas cellars** | Boticas' *vinho dos mortos* | Where the elders buried the Wine of the Dead. |

### Lume
Lume is not mana. It is **memory that burns**.
- A village that holds its festivals, bakes in one oven and buries its dead with names makes the Lume strong.
- Forgetting starves it. Drowning it is worse.
- **Drowned Lume** cannot burn, so it rots into violet **ash-glass**. That is where Vorthak power comes from.
- **Buried Lume**, sealed in clay jars under a cellar floor, keeps. That is the Wine of the Dead.

### The First Oath
The first Jardas, a shepherd whose name is lost, struck a bargain with the land:
1. Lume may be awakened.
2. Lume may be guided.
3. Lume may be defended.
4. Lume may never be *owned*.

Every Ascension war began when a realm tried to break the fourth line.

## 3. The factions

Every faction is right about something. None of them are simply evil.

### The Barrosan Clans (player faction)
*"What the village carries, no king can take."*

Stone villages bound by communal law: shared ovens, shared pasture, shared bulls. They elect their leaders at the village council, fight in family blocks, and settle feuds with the **Chega de Bois**, the clash of two villages' bulls, instead of blood. They are slow to anger and impossible to move. They are also proud, suspicious of outsiders, and quietly guilty: it was a Barrosan Jardas, seventy-seven years ago, who failed.

- **Hero: the Jardas.** The player's named hero, carrier of the Lume, who leads from the front. (The unit shown in battle is titled *Jardas of Salto*.)
- **Workers: Highland Workers.** Shepherds, charcoal-burners and quarrymen.
- **Soldiers.** *Clan Levy*, *Stoneward Spears*, *Crag Archers*, and *Garrano Outriders* on the wild ponies of the Gerês. Heavy blows come from the *Anvil Breakers*, the village smiths who march with sledgehammers.
- **Buildings.** The *Clanhold* keep, the *War Hall*, and the *Clan Croft* with its smoke-house and garden plot.

### The Lioraen Concord
*"We were promised you would remember us."*

The living groves of the springs. The Barrosans call them fairy-folk and leave bread at the fountains. The truth, revealed in Act II: they are the **Mouras Encantadas**, the enchanted guardians that highland legend says sleep beneath fountains, guarding gold that turns to coal in greedy hands. The "gold" is Lume. The "Concord" is their half of the First Oath: they guard the Lume, and the villages remember them. The villages stopped remembering. The Mouras are fading into trees. That is why they fight.

- **Hero: the Warden Ilduara**, a Moura who has not slept in three hundred years.
- **Units.** *Seedkeepers*, *Bloomdancers*, *Rootwardens*, *Thornrunners*, *Windstriders*, *Canopy Menders*, and the *Thornthrower*, a living catapult.

### The Vorthak Cabal
*"You drowned us. We learned to burn underwater."*

The raiders of Act I. Violet ash-glass armour, thralls in chains, hounds that should not exist. They look like monsters, and they are what's left of the village of **Furna**, drowned by the Dominion's first dam seventy-seven years ago. Their Lume rotted into ash-glass and it rotted them with it. Their captain is the saga's most important rival.

- **Malrec, the Ash-Masked.** He was once **Malrique of Furna, the Jardas of the seventy-sixth Ascension**. He failed to stop the drowning, and the Lume in him turned to glass. He wants the new Jardas's Lume to heal his people. He will burn Salto to get it, and he will cry while he does it.

### The Aurean Dominion (the Sunspear Dominion's saga identity)
*"Every Ascension, a war. Every war, a graveyard. We are building a world without either."*

The southern empire of bronze and sun, which won the seventy-fifth Ascension and never gave it back. Its engineers build **great dams**, walls of stone and bronze, to drown the highland springs. The Dominion is not greedy. It has done the arithmetic: each Ascension has killed more people than the last. If the Lume is drowned forever, there will be no seventy-eighth war.

- **Leader: the Sun Regent Aurelia Vess.** In battle, her armies are led by Sun Legates. She is patient and brilliant, and she is not wrong. Her dams will also drown every highland village and turn the Lume into ash-glass.

### The Compaña (the Hollow Legion's saga identity)
*"Candle in the hand, the road beneath the feet."*

The procession of the dead that walks the highland roads at night, candles lit, a living person forced to carry the cross at the front. In the saga they are the dead whose memory was lost or drowned. They walk until someone remembers their names. They are terrifying, and they are also *the saddest people in the world*.

- **Leader: the Candle-King**, the oldest of the forgotten dead.
- **At the front: the Cross-Bearer.** A living woman who has carried the cross for nineteen years: **Leonor of Salto, the Jardas's mother**, who everyone said drowned.

### The Careto Host (the Frostborn Jarls' saga identity)
*"Winter must be chased out. So must the dead."*

Masked revellers in fringed red-and-green suits with iron cowbells, who run down from the Larouco at the end of winter. They seem to be chaos, but they are the oldest wardens of the border between the living and the dead. They drive the Compaña back into the ground each spring, and they have not managed it for years.

- **Leader: O Velho, the Eldest Mask.** A trickster who knows the truth about the Jardas from the first moment and says it only in riddles.

### The Wolfveil Clans (the Wyldkin's saga identity)
*"Every seventh son hears the moon."*

The seventh sons of highland families, who run with the wolves under the full moon. They are feared and hunted, and they are loyal only to the pack. They remember the wolf-trap walls: centuries of their kin were driven into them.

- **Leader: Sétimo, the Seventh.** He will side with whoever frees the wolves from the traps.

### The Granitborn (the Karak Dwarves' saga identity)
*"Stone forgets nothing."*

The people of the castros, the Iron Age hillforts. They let the Lume turn them slowly to granite so that they could remember every Ascension. Their *Thane-Lord Carvalho* is the one who shows the Jardas the ledger of the dead, and the truth about the cycle.

### The Ironmaw Horde (the Grimtusk Horde's saga identity)
*"The Dominion put iron in our mouths. We kept it."*

Miners worked to death in the Dominion's Lume-iron pits, who broke their chains and took the iron into their flesh. Tusked, huge and loud, they are a slave revolt that became a nation. The Dominion calls them beasts.

- **Leader: Warboss Brasa.** Brasa can be the Jardas's most loyal ally or a vengeful enemy, depending on the Act IV choice.

### The Moura Court (the Sylvan Court's saga identity)
*"Every bargain has a price in gold that turns to coal."*

The Mouras who refused to fade. They bartered their Lume for immortality with the Dominion and became silver-eyed, perfect and cold. They are the Lioraen's lost sisters and their deepest shame.

## 4. The Jardas

The player names and creates the hero at the Hero Forge. In the saga:
- They are nineteen, the orphaned grandchild of **Avó Brites**, Salto's wise woman. She was born in Furna and fled to Salto as a girl when the valley was drowned; Malrique of Furna, the failed Jardas who became Malrec, is her elder brother. She brews the *queimada* and speaks the *esconjuro* over it every witch-night.
- Their mother **Leonor** vanished in the drowning of the lower pastures when they were an infant.
- In the prologue, the Vorthak raid Salto. The hero is struck down at the Spring of the Seven Mouths, and the spring blazes. The villagers see them stand up with fire in their eyes. They believe the Lume *saved* them.
- It did not. **The hero died at the spring.** The Lume ascended *into a dead body*, because the dead remember best. The Jardas is Ascendant in the oldest sense: they rose.

Clues are planted from the first battle:
- Wolves go quiet when the Jardas passes.
- The Compaña never attacks the Jardas personally.
- The Careto Eldest Mask greets the Jardas as *"cousin of the road"*.
- Avó Brites never lets the Jardas see their reflection in the spring.

## 5. The campaign: five Acts, forty battles

The campaign map is divided into Acts. Each Act has a main road of battles and **side roads**, optional battles that unlock off the main path, grant rewards, and hold the seven jars of **Wine of the Dead**. Finding all seven unlocks the true ending.

### Act I: Ashes over Salto
*The Vorthak raid. The Jardas rises. Nothing is what it seems.*
1. **The Spring of Seven Mouths.** Drive the Vorthak raiders from Salto on the night of the Ascension.
2. **The Burning Oven.** The raiders fire the communal oven, and the village's shared bread and memory with it. Retake the square.
3. **Garrano Run.** Chase Malrec's outriders across the high pastures before they reach the pass.
4. **The Wolf-Trap Walls** (side road). A pack of Wolfveil is trapped by Vorthak hounds in the old stone wolf-trap. Free them and the wolves remember. *Jar of Wine I.*
5. **Ashfen Mire.** Follow the ash trail into the bog where nothing should burn.
6. **Tourém Crossing.** The smugglers' town has sold the Jardas's location to both sides. Hold the bridge.
7. **The Night of the Witches** (side road). Montalto's witch-night is overrun by the Cabal. Protect the esconjuro so the queimada is spoken. *Jar of Wine II.*
8. **Malrec's Pyre.** Break the Vorthak camp. **Twist:** Malrec removes his mask. His face is half glass and half highland farmer. He calls the Jardas by their mother's name.

**Act I ending:** Malrec escapes with one line. *"Ask your grandmother why the spring never shows your face."*

### Act II: The Drowned Villages
*The water rises in the valleys. The Mouras wake. The Dominion arrives.*
1. **The First Dam.** Dominion engineers are surveying the Cávado. Scatter them.
2. **Grove of Seven Fountains.** The Lioraen attack on sight. They think the Jardas is a thief.
3. **Where the Bread Was Left.** Defend a spring shrine from Dominion soldiers alongside the Lioraen. **Reveal:** the Lioraen are the Mouras Encantadas.
4. **The Candle Road** (side road). Face the Compaña for the first time. Their line splits around the Jardas without touching them. *Jar of Wine III.*
5. **Furna Below the Water.** At low water, fight among the drowned roofs of Furna, birthplace of the Vorthak.
6. **The Regent's Envoy.** Aurelia Vess offers peace: give up the Lume and the dams will stop. Refuse, and her legions march.
7. **The Silver Sisters** (side road). The Moura Court, the Mouras who sold themselves to the Dominion, come for their sisters. *Jar of Wine IV.*
8. **Rising Water.** Hold the Seven Fountains while the Dominion opens the sluices. The grove survives only if the Jardas holds.

**Act II ending:** Ilduara, dying into a tree, touches the Jardas's face. *"You are cold, little one. The living are never this cold."*

### Act III: Wine of the Dead
*The truth. The dead. The mother.*
1. **Boticas Cellars.** The elders buried the Lume-wine here in the last war. The Compaña guards it.
2. **The Eldest Mask.** The Careto Host blocks the Larouco. Beat them, and O Velho finally speaks plainly.
3. **The Castro Ledger.** At Carvalhelhos, the Granitborn show the ledger of the dead. The Jardas's name is in it, dated the night of the Ascension. **Twist: the Jardas died at the spring.**
4. **Seventh Son** (side road). Sétimo's pack is hunted by the Dominion. Save them and the Wolfveil swear to the Jardas. *Jar of Wine V.*
5. **The Candle Road, Again.** Hunt the Compaña to its heart.
6. **The Cross-Bearer.** The living woman at the front of the procession is **Leonor, the Jardas's mother**. She has carried the cross for nineteen years, walking beside her own child's death, waiting.
7. **The Pilgrims' Bridge** (side road). Escort the freed dead across the old stone bridge. *Jar of Wine VI.*
8. **Chase Out the Winter.** Fight alongside the Caretos to drive the Compaña back into the ground and free Leonor. **Twist:** Leonor refuses to put the cross down. If she does, her child goes with the dead.

**Act III ending:** Avó Brites confesses. She knew. She spoke the esconjuro over her dead grandchild and the Lume answered. She would do it again.

### Act IV: The Rabagão Wall
*War against the Dominion. The choice that splits the world.*
1. **The Geira Road.** March south on the ancient road and wake the milestone Lume-stones.
2. **Ironmaw Rising.** The Ironmaw Horde revolts in the Dominion's mines. Fight them, or beside them.
3. **The Bronze Legions.** Meet the Dominion army in open battle.
4. **Chega de Bois** (side road). Two villages' war-bulls, for the old honour of the valley. Win, and the Barrosã bulls march with you. *Jar of Wine VII.*
5. **Malrec's Last Offer.** Malrec returns, dying. Take his glass heart and the Vorthak join you, or leave him his dignity.
6. **The Sun Regent.** Break Aurelia Vess's field army. Captured, she shows you the numbers: the dead of every Ascension, rising each time.
7. **The Wall.** Stand on top of the Rabagão Wall with the Lume in your hands, and choose. The Act ends with one of two final battles:
   - **7A. Break the Wall.** Free the drowned Lume. The flood takes the lowland towns, and the Dominion never forgives.
   - **7B. Seize the Wall.** Hold the dam and the Lume both, as Malrec always said you must. The highlands are safe, and you have broken the fourth line of the Oath.

### Act V: The Seventy-Seventh Ascension
*The Lume wakes fully and wants to become a realm of its own.*

**The final reveal:** the Lume is not a power at all. It is **every highland person who ever lived**, and it wants to *ascend*: to become a Realm and draw the living into memory with it. The seventy-six earlier Jardas each stopped it and died doing so. The Ascension wars were the realms fighting over who would get to carry the thing that would kill them.

1. **The Burning Geira.** The milestones flare. The dead walk in daylight.
2. **Montalto Besieged.** Every faction the Jardas wronged comes for them at once.
3. **The Moura's Last Spring.** Restore the last Lioraen fountain, or let it die, depending on earlier choices.
4. **The Glass Choir.** Every ash-glass creature rises, singing in Furna's voices.
5. **Salto Remembers.** Return home. The village is empty, and the oven is warm.
6. **The Spring of Seven Mouths.** Face the **Ascendant**, the Lume's avatar, wearing the Jardas's own face.
7. **The Seventy-Seventh Oath.** The final battle. Its ending depends on the Act IV choice and the Wine of the Dead:
   - **Break-the-Wall ending, "The Oath Kept."** The Jardas walks into the spring and takes the Lume with them into the Compaña. Leonor finally puts down the cross. Salto remembers two names.
   - **Seize-the-Wall ending, "The Ascendant Realm."** The Jardas becomes the Lume and rules the highlands forever, alive and not alive. The eightieth Ascension will be a war against *them*.
   - **True ending (all seven jars), "The Chega."** The Jardas pours the Wine of the Dead into the spring and the whole highland remembers at once. No single person carries the Lume: every village carries a share, like the common pasture and the shared bull. There is no Jardas and no Ascension, and there will be no seventy-eighth war. The last scene is Avó Brites speaking the esconjuro, and the fire answering from every hearth in the Terras Frias.

## 6. How the story is told in play

- **Campaign briefings:** each battle opens with a written chronicle page: what happened, who you face, what they want.
- **Battle voices:** at the start of each campaign battle a line from the Jardas or an ally sets the scene. The enemy commander speaks at three and eight minutes, taunting, pleading, or telling the truth too early.
- **Victory chronicles:** each win ends with a short paragraph moving the story forward, shown on the result ledger.
- **Side roads:** optional battles with the jars of Wine of the Dead, tracked on the campaign map.
- **The choice:** the Rabagão Wall offers two final battles. Taking one seals the other.

## 7. Length

- 39 battles on the campaign map: 31 on the main road (one of the two Wall battles is played) and 7 side roads.
- At 15 to 25 minutes a battle, the campaign alone is roughly 10 to 16 hours before replays, skirmish or harder difficulties.
- Difficulty rises from Easy in Act I to Brutal through Acts IV and V.
- Growth path: each Act has room for more side roads, and the other seven factions can become playable campaigns of their own, each telling the same Ascension from their side.

## 8. Tone rules for future writing

- Nobody is purely evil. Everyone lost someone to the Lume.
- Highland warmth first: bread, fire, bulls, grandmothers. Then horror.
- Plant every twist at least twice before it lands.
- Keep lines short. The best line in the saga is five words long: *"The living are never this cold."*
